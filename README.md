# Mauritius Civic Technology & Digital Democracy

**Independent civic technology and public-interest digital governance work in Mauritius.**

This repository documents an evolving set of initiatives to make legislation, public consultation, and democratic participation more accessible. It is independent of any fellowship application, political party, or external funder.

## Background

The public-facing civic consultation platform [Mauritius.Name](https://mauritius.name) provides a space to examine proposed legislation, publish alternative drafting ideas, and invite public comments. This repository is a companion for documentation and, where reviewed for safe release, reusable open-source components. **It is not currently a mirror of the production website.**

## Workstreams

| Workstream | Scope | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legislative consultation | Public reading, comments and suggested amendments to bills | Public platform in operation; source release pending review |
| Constitutional renewal | Public discussion of constitutional reform proposals | Consultation materials developed; source release pending review |
| Access to information | Draft proposals on freedom of information and open data | Proposal and consultation work; not enacted law |
| Water rights | Proposed modernisation of water governance legislation | Draft proposal for public discussion |
| Inclusive participation | Proposed positive measures and equal opportunity reforms | Drafting and consultation work |
| Digital trust | Research into consent, transparency and accountable technology | Related but separate [Global Digital Trust Commons](https://github.com/dkissoondoyal/global-digital-trust-commons) project |

These are civic proposals and technical initiatives, **not official government publications or statements of enacted law**.

## Project principles

- **Public interest:** make complex policy issues easier to understand and discuss.
- **Accessibility:** support participation across languages, devices and abilities.
- **Transparency:** distinguish original legislation, commentary, and proposed amendments.
- **Privacy and security:** review all code and data before publication; do not expose personal submissions, credentials or production configuration.
- **Independence:** document work accurately without implying endorsement by public institutions.
